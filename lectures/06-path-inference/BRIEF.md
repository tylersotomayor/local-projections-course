## 1. Session brief

**Opening situation.**
A referee is reading two sentences. The first is from Inoue, Jordà, and Kuersteiner's shelter-inflation application (their Figure 4): "the response of shelter PCEPI is almost uniformly negative each month over the 4 years displayed", written under a figure whose 48 plotted pointwise Newey–West intervals (months 0–47) all contain zero; the unplotted month-48 interval, $[-7.201,-0.035]$, is the one Lecture 5's Researcher A called significant, and it is the only one of the 49 estimated that excludes zero (REP03 CSV). The second is from Jordà and Taylor's unemployment application (their Figure 6a): the unemployment rate "gradually increases to about 1¼ percent approximately two years after impact, and returns back to zero about four years after impact", written under a figure whose pointwise bands exclude zero at 13 of 49 months and whose joint test rejects "no response at any horizon" with $\chi^2(49)=446.9$. The referee's question is the lecture's question: which of these sentences does the evidence support, and which procedure would have to be run to know? The spine's shorter version opens the notes: "the response is negative throughout the first year" is one sentence about five quarterly horizons (thirteen monthly ones), and a pointwise band was built to make one statement at a time.

**Decision or empirical question.**
Given a stacked vector of estimates $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=(\hat\beta_0,\dots,\hat\beta_H)'$ and a horizon set $\mathcal H$ named by a claim, which null hypothesis does the claim state, which statistic tests it, and what does the corresponding band or test promise in repeated samples? Six claims recur: (i) "nonzero at month $h$" (pointwise interval); (ii) "negative at every month in $\mathcal H$" (for an $\mathcal H$ fixed before estimation, one-sided pointwise tests at level $\alpha$ at every month in $\mathcal H$, combined by the intersection-union rule of §6 D10; the one-sided sup-$t$ band of D25 is valid but conservative for this claim); (iii) "different from zero somewhere in $\mathcal H$" (Wald test); (iv) "lower on average over $\mathcal H$" (one linear combination); (v) "the cumulative effect through $h$ is $B_h$" (accumulated response, a linear combination with the full covariance); (vi) "curve A differs from curve B" (a direct test with the cross-curve covariance). Simultaneous bands (Bonferroni or sup-$t$) are required for the claims readers make after looking: an $\mathcal H$ chosen after seeing the band, "which months are nonzero", and shape claims such as the peak date or where the sign changes. Claims (iii)–(vi) and the sup-$t$ band need the off-diagonal elements of $\boldsymbol\Sigma$, the object Lecture 5 ignored; (i), (ii) with a pre-specified $\mathcal H$, and Bonferroni do not.

**Target student and prerequisites.**
The student has completed Lecture 5 (coverage, Newey–West, the score $s_t u_{t,h}$, lag augmentation), Lecture 2 (cumulative response $B_h$, common sample $\mathcal T_H$), Lecture 3 (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell partialling out), and Lecture 4 (LP-IV with `ivregress 2sls`); system `gmm` is introduced here for the stacked covariance. Mathematical prerequisites: variance of a linear combination $\boldsymbol\ell'\boldsymbol\Sigma\boldsymbol\ell$, the multivariate normal, and the $\chi^2$ distribution of a quadratic form; the bivariate normal is used once in the scalar case and stated when needed. Stata prerequisites: `regress`, `newey`, `predict, resid`, a `forvalues` loop over horizons, matrices via `matrix` and a short Mata block that the lab project supplies.

**Learning outcomes (five).**
1. Translate a sentence about a response path into a null hypothesis over a named horizon set $\mathcal H$ and choose the procedure that tests it: a pointwise interval, one-sided pointwise tests combined by the intersection-union rule, a simultaneous band, a Wald test, or a single linear combination.
2. Estimate the cross-horizon covariance $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ of $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ by partialling out the controls, stacking the horizon-specific scores $\tilde s_t\tilde u_{t,h}$, and applying one HAC estimator to the stack; verify the construction against Stata's system `gmm` with `vce(hac nw m)` to numerical precision.
3. Compute pointwise, Bonferroni, and sup-$t$ bands from $(\hat{\boldsymbol\beta},\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma})$, explain why $c_{1-\alpha}\ge z_{1-\alpha/2}$ and why the sup-$t$ band is always narrower than Bonferroni, and much narrower when horizons are correlated, and state what each band's coverage promises.
4. Compute the standard error of any linear combination of horizons — an accumulated response, an average over a window, a difference between two curves — from the full covariance, and show numerically what the sum-of-variances shortcut and the overlapping-bands heuristic get wrong.
5. Read Inoue–Jordà–Kuersteiner significance bands as a test under the null of no response at all, distinguish them from confidence bands, and say which of the two the referee's sentence needs.

**Smallest useful model.**
Two horizons of the Lecture 1 economy on a common sample. The horizon-$h$ regressions are $y_{t}=\mu_0+\beta_0 s_t+\gamma_0 y_{t-1}+u_{t,0}$ and $y_{t+1}=\mu_1+\beta_1 s_t+\gamma_1 y_{t-1}+u_{t,1}$ with $u_{t,0}=v_t$ and $u_{t,1}=\theta_0 s_{t+1}+v_{t+1}+\rho v_t$. Because $s_t$ is unpredictable, $\sqrt T(\hat\beta_h-\beta_h)\approx T^{-1/2}\sum_t s_t u_{t,h}/\sigma_s^2$, so
$$\boldsymbol\Sigma\approx\frac{1}{T\sigma_s^4}\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_s^2\sigma_v^2 & \rho\,\sigma_s^2\sigma_v^2\\ \rho\,\sigma_s^2\sigma_v^2 & \sigma_s^2\{\theta_0^2\sigma_s^2+\sigma_v^2(1+\rho^2)\}\end{pmatrix},$$
and the two estimates are correlated through the shared $v_t$ even though each score is serially uncorrelated (Lecture 5's point). With $\varrho=\operatorname{Corr}(\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1)$, the probability that two pointwise 95% intervals both cover is $P(|Z_0|\le1.96,|Z_1|\le1.96)$ for a standard bivariate normal with correlation $\varrho$: 0.9025 at $\varrho=0$, 0.9182 at $\varrho=0.733$ (the Figure 3 design's $(0,1)$ correlation), 0.95 only at $|\varrho|=1$. The sup-$t$ critical value is the $c$ that makes this probability 0.95: 2.2365 at $\varrho=0$ (equal to Šidák), 2.1719 at $\varrho=0.733$, 1.96 at $\varrho=1$; Bonferroni uses 2.2414 regardless. Everything in the general case is this picture with $H+1$ coordinates.

**Dependency chain of sections** (spine order preserved; titles refined; the scalar case lives inside section 2).

1. `#sec-l06-three-claims` *Three claims about one curve.* The referee's sentences are nulls over a horizon set $\mathcal H$; "negative at month 6", "negative at every month in $\mathcal H$", and "different somewhere in $\mathcal H$" are three hypotheses, and a table matches each to the procedure that tests it and to the object the procedure needs. Month 6 takes a pointwise interval. "Negative at every month" of an $\mathcal H$ fixed before estimation takes one-sided pointwise tests at level $\alpha$ at every month, the intersection-union rule (§6 D10), which needs no off-diagonal element; the one-sided sup-$t$ band is valid but conservative for it. "Different somewhere" takes a Wald test. The table's last rows are the claims that require a simultaneous band: an $\mathcal H$ read off the band after estimation, "which months are nonzero", and shape claims (peak date, sign change).
2. `#sec-l06-joint-distribution` *The joint distribution of the stacked estimates.* Two horizons of the Lecture 1 economy show that $\hat\beta_0$ and $\hat\beta_1$ share $v_t$ and are correlated; the general statement $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\approx N(\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\Sigma)$ follows by stacking the scores $\tilde s_t\tilde u_{t,h}$ after partialling out the controls, and $\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}=\hat{\boldsymbol\Psi}/(T\hat q^2)$ with one Bartlett HAC estimator on the stack is the same matrix Stata's system `gmm` reports with `vce(hac nw m)`.
3. `#sec-l06-simultaneous-bands` *Pointwise versus simultaneous coverage.* The probability that all $|\mathcal H|$ pointwise intervals cover falls from 0.95 toward $0.95^{|\mathcal H|}$ as correlation weakens (0.51 for 13 independent horizons); Bonferroni restores family-wise coverage by dividing $\alpha$, and the sup-$t$ band restores it with the smallest constant $c_{1-\alpha}$ that the estimated $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ allows, computed by the three-step plug-in algorithm.
4. `#sec-l06-joint-tests` *Joint tests over a horizon set.* The Wald statistic $W_{\mathcal H}=\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_{\mathcal H}'\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\mathcal H}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_{\mathcal H}\sim\chi^2_{|\mathcal H|}$ answers "different somewhere" and nothing about where; with many highly correlated horizons it is also numerically fragile. Its $\chi^2$ reference is unreliable too, because HAC Wald tests with many restrictions relative to the sample over-reject. In the null Figure 3 design with 137 rows the nominal 5% test rejects 16.8% of the time at $|\mathcal H|=3$ and 84.4% at $|\mathcal H|=13$ (§6 D11). The $\chi^2$ p-values for 25–49 restrictions at $N=121$ (anchor C) are therefore not trustworthy, and the shelter Wald statistics, which D11 does not calibrate, carry the same warning. A single linear combination $\boldsymbol\ell'\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ — an average over the window — is the joint statement to report, with its smaller distortion named (11.0% in the same design).
5. `#sec-l06-curve-differences` *Differences between curves.* Two responses to the same intervention have covariance $\boldsymbol\Sigma^{YX}$, so $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta^Y_h-\hat\beta^X_h)=\Sigma^{YY}_{hh}+\Sigma^{XX}_{hh}-2\Sigma^{YX}_{hh}$; overlapping bands and star-counting ignore the third term and are not tests.
6. `#sec-l06-accumulated` *Accumulated responses.* $\operatorname{Var}(\hat B_h)=\boldsymbol\iota'\boldsymbol\Sigma_{0:h}\boldsymbol\iota$ needs every covariance below the diagonal. One regression on a common sample delivers the same number: the long difference when the summed responses are of a period change (shelter inflation), the accumulated-level LP of $\sum_{j\le h}y_{t+j}$ when they are of a level (a multiplier). A cumulative multiplier, a ratio of two accumulated-level responses, inherits the whole matrix.
7. `#sec-l06-significance-bands` *Significance bands.* Under the null of no response at any horizon the estimator's variance simplifies to $\sigma^{0\,2}_h=g_h/\gamma_{sz}^2$ with $g_h$ the long-run variance of $z_ty_{t+h}$, so a band around zero can be drawn from an auxiliary regression of $\xi_{t,h}=z_ty_{t+h}$ on a constant; it answers "is there any response?", not "how precise is $\hat\beta_h$?".
8. `#sec-l06-evidence` *Evidence: a known economy and two published paths.* IJK Figure 3 (Newey–West versus sup-$t$ in the known economy), the shelter path (three bands, one Wald test, one sentence), and the unemployment path (13 pointwise stars, zero sup-$t$ exclusions, a Wald statistic whose $\chi^2(49)$ p-value is not trustworthy at $N=121$, and an average over months 12–36 that is 2.35 standard errors from zero, the joint statement reported). On the shelter path the significance band is drawn on the path's own controls (§6 D7), and the path leaves it at no month.
9. `#sec-l06-summary` *What the lecture built and what it leaves open.* One covariance matrix answers every claim about the path; the next lecture asks why two estimators of the same path can disagree in a sample.

**Likely glossary terms** (owned keys; `horizon-set` and `linear-combination-test` approved by D22 and listed in `terminology-plan.md`; definition drafts).
- `cross-horizon-covariance` — The off-diagonal part of the covariance matrix $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ of the stacked LP estimates: how the sampling error of $\hat\beta_h$ moves with that of $\hat\beta_k$. It is nonzero because the horizon-$h$ and horizon-$k$ regressions share the same intervention dates and overlapping future shocks, and it is what every claim about more than one horizon needs.
- `stacked-regression` — The $H+1$ horizon-specific local projections written as one system, estimated jointly (by system GMM or by stacking the partialled-out scores) so that one covariance matrix for all horizons can be computed. Coefficients equal the equation-by-equation OLS estimates on a common sample; only the covariance is new.
- `pointwise-band` — The set of horizon-by-horizon confidence intervals $\hat\beta_h\pm z_{1-\alpha/2}\hat\sigma_h$. Each interval covers its own $\beta_h$ with probability $1-\alpha$; the band as a whole covers the path with a lower, unreported probability.
- `simultaneous-band` — A band constructed so that the probability that it contains $\beta_h$ at every $h$ in $\mathcal H$ at once is at least $1-\alpha$. It supports sentences about the whole path, including horizon sets and shapes read off the estimates; its width is the price of that support.
- `sup-t-band` — The simultaneous band $\hat\beta_h\pm c_{1-\alpha}\hat\sigma_h$ whose constant $c_{1-\alpha}$ is the $1-\alpha$ quantile of $\max_h|Z_h|/\sigma_h$ for $\mathbf Z\sim N(\mathbf 0,\boldsymbol\Sigma)$ (Montiel Olea and Plagborg-Møller 2019). It is the narrowest band of this shape with simultaneous coverage $1-\alpha$, and it is never narrower than the pointwise band.
- `bonferroni-band` — The simultaneous band that uses $z_{1-\alpha/(2|\mathcal H|)}$ in place of $z_{1-\alpha/2}$. It guarantees family-wise coverage without knowing the correlations and pays for the ignorance: for $|\mathcal H|\ge2$ it is always wider than sup-$t$ (even under independence, where sup-$t$ equals Šidák), and much wider when horizons are correlated.
- `multiplicity` — The fact that a claim about several horizons is several hypotheses at once, so that per-hypothesis error rates compound. Thirteen independent 95% intervals cover jointly only 51% of the time.
- `family-wise-error-rate` — The probability that at least one hypothesis in a family is falsely rejected, or that at least one interval in a band misses. Simultaneous bands and joint tests control it; pointwise bands control only the per-horizon rate.
- `joint-hypothesis` — A null that restricts several response coefficients at once, $\mathbf R_{\mathcal H}\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf 0$. "No response during the first year" is a joint hypothesis with $|\mathcal H|$ restrictions; rejecting it says the coefficients are not all zero and does not say which one is not.
- `wald-test` — The test that compares $W_{\mathcal H}=\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_{\mathcal H}'\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\mathcal H}^{-1}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_{\mathcal H}$ with a $\chi^2_{|\mathcal H|}$ critical value. It weights the estimates by their inverse covariance, so it can reject strongly while every pointwise interval contains zero, and it becomes numerically fragile when $\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{\mathcal H}$ is nearly singular.
- `curve-difference-test` — A test of $\beta^Y_h=\beta^X_h$ (at one horizon or over $\mathcal H$) that uses the covariance between the two curves' estimates. Whether the two pointwise bands overlap is not this test: overlap ignores the covariance and uses the wrong critical value.
- `accumulated-response-uncertainty` — The standard error of $\hat B_h=\sum_{j\le h}\hat\beta_j$, $\sqrt{\boldsymbol\iota'\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{0:h}\boldsymbol\iota}$, which includes every covariance among the summed horizons. The sum of variances alone understates it when responses are positively correlated, which they are.
- `significance-band` — A band around zero, $\pm z\,\hat\sigma^0_h$, whose width is computed under the null that the intervention has no effect at any horizon (Inoue, Jordà, and Kuersteiner 2026). A response that leaves it is evidence of some response; the band says nothing about the precision of the estimate that left it.
- `horizon-set` (approved, D22) — The set $\mathcal H$ of horizons a claim names, with $|\mathcal H|$ its size. Writing $\mathcal H$ down before testing is what makes a claim testable; "throughout the first year" at monthly frequency is $\mathcal H=\{0,\dots,12\}$.
- `linear-combination-test` (approved, D22) — A test of one weighted sum $\boldsymbol\ell'\boldsymbol\beta$, such as the average response over a window, with standard error $\sqrt{\boldsymbol\ell'\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}\boldsymbol\ell}$. It answers a narrower question than a Wald test, with one degree of freedom and an interpretable sign.

## 6. Derivations to verify

**D1. Two-horizon covariance (`eq-l06-two-horizon-cov`).** Source: the Lecture 1 economy $y_t=\rho y_{t-1}+\theta_0s_t+v_t$ with $s_t$ i.i.d. and independent of $v$. Steps: (i) substitute forward once: $y_{t+1}=\rho^2y_{t-1}+\rho\theta_0s_t+\theta_0s_{t+1}+v_{t+1}+\rho v_t$, so $\beta_1=\rho\theta_0$ and $u_{t,1}=\theta_0s_{t+1}+v_{t+1}+\rho v_t$; (ii) with $y_{t-1}$ as the only control and $s_t$ unpredictable, $\hat\beta_h-\beta_h\approx(T\sigma_s^2)^{-1}\sum_ts_tu_{t,h}$; (iii) take the covariance of the two sums: $\mathbb E[s_t^2u_{t,0}u_{t,1}]=\sigma_s^2\rho\sigma_v^2$, cross-$t$ terms vanish because each contains an odd power of an independent mean-zero $s$; (iv) $\operatorname{Var}(s_tu_{t,1})=\sigma_s^2(\theta_0^2\sigma_s^2+\sigma_v^2(1+\rho^2))$; (v) divide. Result: $\operatorname{Corr}(\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1)=\rho\sigma_v^2/\sqrt{\sigma_v^2(\theta_0^2\sigma_s^2+\sigma_v^2(1+\rho^2))}$, $=\rho/\sqrt{2+\rho^2}$ at unit parameters. Numerical check: `handcalc3.py`, $T=200$, 4,000 replications, seed 20260913: $\rho=0.5$: MC correlation 0.314 (theory 0.333), $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_0)$ 0.00536 (theory $1/199=0.00503$), $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_1)$ 0.01148 (0.01131), Cov 0.00247 (0.00251); $\rho=0.9$: 0.527 (0.537), 0.00524, 0.01402 (0.01412), 0.00452 (0.00452). Report both, with the finite-sample gap named.

**D5. **D7. Significance band (`eq-l06-sigband`).** Source: IJK eq. (16)–(19), just-identified IV with instrument $z_t$ ($z_t=s_t$ in the shelter case), $n=T-h$. Steps: (i) $\sqrt n(\hat\beta_h-\beta_h)=n^{-1/2}\sum_tz_tu_{t,h}/(n^{-1}\sum_tz_ts_t)$; (ii) under $H_0:\beta_h=0$ the numerator is $n^{-1/2}\sum_tz_ty_{t+h}$ (after partialling out), so its long-run variance $g_h$ can be estimated from $\xi_{t,h}=z_ty_{t+h}$ without using $\hat\beta_h$ — the LM principle; (iii) the denominator converges to $\gamma_{sz}$; (iv) $\sigma^{0\,2}_h=g_h/\gamma_{sz}^2$ and the band is $\pm z_{1-\alpha/(2(H+1))}\sqrt{\hat g_h/n}/\hat\gamma_{sz}$, the Bonferroni factor because the band is read at $H+1$ horizons at once; (v) special case $z=s=y$ white noise: $g=\gamma_y^2$, $\gamma_{sz}=\gamma_y$, $\sigma^0=1$, the $\pm1.96/\sqrt n$ autocorrelogram band. Implementation (archive `Figure4.do` lines 97–131): the partialling-out regressions add 12 lags of housing starts to the pointwise controls (D7); `newey eta_h, lag(48)` gives $\sqrt{\hat g_h/n}$ as `_se[_cons]`; divide by the mean of $\tilde s_t^2$. Numerical check (REP03 CSV `significance_bonferroni`, reproduced with zero difference by `d7/d7_authors.do`): half-widths 0.211 ($h=0$), 1.234 (12), 1.877 (24), 1.931 (36), 2.407 (48), against $|\hat\beta_h|$ of 0.066, 0.225, 1.502, 2.679, 3.618 on the pointwise path. That verdict compares objects with different noise. The band partials out housing starts (on those controls the joint-step s.e. at $h=48$ is 0.750, B1), while the path omits them (s.e. 1.828), so the notes present the authors' "months 31–48" as conditional on mismatched control sets. *Matched band (the lecture's sentence).* The same construction on the B2 control set and common sample (`repair/sigband_matched.do`, StataNow/SE 19.5, 126 s) uses $\xi_{t,h}=\tilde s_t\tilde y_{t+h}$, with both partialled on 12 lags each of $\Delta y_t$, `stir`, `urate` over $T=323$ rows, `newey` lag 48, $\hat q=0.00218645$, and Bonferroni $z_{1-0.05/98}=3.285$ over 49 horizons. **D8. sup-$t$ plug-in algorithm and its Monte Carlo error (`eq-l06-supt`).** Source: MOPM (2019) Theorem/algorithm as restated in IJK §5 and JT §7.2: $P(\boldsymbol\beta\in\hat{\mathbf B}(c))\to P(\max_h|V_h|/\sigma_h\le c)$, $\mathbf V\sim N(\mathbf 0,\boldsymbol\Sigma)$. **D10. "At every month in $\mathcal H$" and the intersection-union rule (`eq-l06-iut`).** Source: Berger (1982). The claim "$\beta_h>0$ for every $h\in\mathcal H$" is the alternative; its null is the union $\bigcup_{h\in\mathcal H}\{\beta_h\le0\}$. Rule: assert the claim when every one-sided level-$\alpha$ test rejects, $\hat\beta_h/\hat\sigma_h>z_{1-\alpha}$ for all $h\in\mathcal H$ (reverse the signs for "negative"). Steps: (i) under the null some $h^*\in\mathcal H$ has $\beta_{h^*}\le0$; (ii) the event that the rule asserts is contained in the event that the test at $h^*$ rejects; (iii) so the error probability is at most $P(\hat\beta_{h^*}/\hat\sigma_{h^*}>z_{1-\alpha})\le\alpha$ asymptotically, for any $\boldsymbol\Sigma$ and any $|\mathcal H|$. No multiplicity adjustment and no off-diagonal element enters. Asserting only when every two-sided 95% interval lies on one side of zero has level 0.025. The one-sided sup-$t$ band (D25) uses $c\ge z_{1-\alpha}$, so it is valid but conservative for this claim. The guarantee needs $\mathcal H$ fixed before estimation. When $\mathcal H$ is read off the band (for example, the months whose pointwise intervals exclude zero), and for "which months are nonzero", "different somewhere" or shape claims (peak date, sign change), a simultaneous band or a joint test is required. Numerical check (`repair/iut_check.py`, NumPy, seed 20260913, $10^6$ draws) with D4's block: draw $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}_{0:2}\sim N((0.5,0.75,0)',\hat{\boldsymbol\Sigma}_{0:2})$, so "positive at $h=0,1,2$" is false at $h^*=2$. The share of draws whose three lower 95% bounds all exceed zero is 0.02504 (MC s.e. 0.00016), identical to the share at $h=2$ alone, and it cannot exceed $P(\text{the bound at }h^*\text{ excludes zero})=0.025$ beyond Monte Carlo error. Assert share $\le0.025+3\times0.00016$.
